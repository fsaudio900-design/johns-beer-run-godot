# v2.19: talking to Ben, your way

**Download:** grab **JohnsBeerRun-Setup-v2.19.exe** (installer) or **JohnsBeerRun-Windows-v2.19.zip** below.

### Ben's conversations now use Dialogic, with choices
Talking to Ben now opens a dialogue box with his lines and John's, and you choose what John says. Click a choice, or advance the text with E, Enter, Space or a left click.

**Ben's job offer branches:**
- **Ask what the job is:** Ben explains (rob the Fuel Stop, bring him the money). Then you can:
  - **"Sure, I'm in."** Ben is delighted: "That's my guy! I knew I could count on you."
  - **"Not tonight, Ben."** Ben is gracious: "No worries. The offer stands. Knock if you change your mind."
  - **"Why don't you do it yourself?"** Ben gets passive-aggressive: "Some of us have reputations to keep. Not everyone gets to just... be you." Then you either take the job ("Wow. Thank you SO much. I'm touched. Really.") or turn him down ("I'll just sit here. Broke. Alone. Don't worry about me. You never do.").
- **Be rude from the start** ("Make it quick, Ben. My beer's getting warm."): Ben stays polite in name only, and you can take the job or say you're not interested.

**Turning him down and coming back:**
- You can refuse the job and come back any time. Knock again and Ben asks if you've changed your mind.
- If you were nice, he's friendly about it. If you were rude, he greets you with "Oh. It's you. Here to let me down again?"
- You can apologise ("Sorry about before. I'm in.") to get back on his good side.

**Ben remembers your tone:**
- His reminders, the hand-over and his goodbye lines depend on whether you were nice or rude.
  - Nice: "Ha! Look at that! You beautiful lunatic."
  - Rude: "Huh. You actually did it. I'm almost proud."
- Short-change him and he'll notice: "That's it? Where's the rest of it, John?"
- If John robbed the store before talking to Ben, Ben spots the cash and offers to take it off his hands.

### For editing
- The conversation lives in `dialogue/ben.dtl`, with characters `dialogue/Ben.dch` and `dialogue/John.dch`. Open them in Godot's **Dialogic** tab to change the lines or add new branches.

Built and tested on Godot 4.7.2.
